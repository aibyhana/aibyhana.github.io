Testing myself teaches more than rereading does. This is called the testing effect.

In a study by Roediger and Karpicke (2006), students either read a passage several times or read it once and then wrote down what they could remember. Right afterwards the rereaders did a little better. A week later the students who had practised recalling remembered clearly more.

Rereading feels good because the text looks familiar. Familiar is not the same as being able to produce it.

## How I use it

- Close the book and write down what the chapter said.
- Turn headings into questions and answer them from memory.
- Explain it out loud: the [[Feynman technique]].
- Put the questions on a schedule: [[Spaced repetition]].
